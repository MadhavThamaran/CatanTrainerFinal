# Counting to 15

The game ends the moment a player reaches **15 victory points on their own turn**. The points come from:

- a settlement: 1, and a city: 2 (upgrading a settlement adds 1)
- Longest Road: 2, and Largest Army: 2
- victory-point cards: 1 each, hidden

Count both sides. Your total is exact; theirs is the visible total plus whatever development cards they may be hiding. Then ask the practical question: how many turns does each of us need? Compare what each player can actually afford over the next few rolls, not just the gap in points.

---
# Awards are four-point swings

Longest Road needs a chain of **5 or more** roads and has to be **strictly longer** than your opponent's to take it; a tie leaves it with the current holder. Largest Army needs **3** played knights and follows the same strictly-more rule.

Taking an award from the other player gives you two points *and* removes two from them: a four-point swing, worth far more than the single point of a city.

Roads can be cut. A settlement on a corner in the middle of your opponent's road breaks it there, so a long chain can drop below 5 in one move. Before you chase an award, check whether one well-placed settlement could take it away again.

---
# Block or sprint

Once you can count both sides, the choice is usually clear.

**If you're ahead in the count, sprint.** Spend on the points that cost the fewest turns, such as the settlement you can afford or the city you are one card from, and don't spend a turn on a block that doesn't change the count.

**If you're behind, blocking buys you turns.** A robber on their best hex, a road cut that costs them an award, or a steal that leaves them a card short of their build all slow their count without you having to out-build them. Pick the block that costs them the most progress, not the one that merely looks aggressive.

When the counts are close, the dice decide the next turn, so keep your hand at 9 or fewer. A 7 should be a nuisance, not the game.
