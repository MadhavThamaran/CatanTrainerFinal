# What a 7 does

A 7 pays no one. Instead, in this order:

1. Every player holding **more than 9 cards** discards half their hand, rounded down. This is not the standard-game limit of 7: here a hand of 10 loses 5 and a hand of 12 loses 6.
2. The player who rolled moves the robber to a different hex.
3. They steal one random card from a player with a building on that hex, if that player has any cards and isn't protected (see page 3).

The robber blocks the hex it sits on: while it's there that hex pays nobody. A 7 comes up about one roll in six, and a played knight card moves the robber too.

Everything about robber play comes from those three steps: what you're holding when a 7 lands, where the robber goes, and whose card it takes.

---
# Don't hold ten cards

When a 7 lands, anyone above 9 cards loses half of them, and you don't choose when. Ending your turn with 10 or more cards is a gamble at roughly one in six.

Make the hand limit part of your turn: before you end it, spend down. Build, buy a development card, or bank-trade a surplus into something you'll need. The cards you were saving for "next turn" are exactly the ones a 7 takes.

If you do have to discard, you choose which cards go, so keep the ones that complete your next build and give up the singles you can't use. The limit applies to your opponent too: their hand size is visible, and a 10-card hand is one 7 away from losing half.

---
# Where to put the robber

Think "deny their engine", not "take their cards". The block matters more than the steal: a hex that supplies the resource they are short of, or one of their high-pip corners, costs them income every turn it stays put. Prefer a hex that doesn't touch your own buildings.

The steal is one random card, so it's worth more against a big hand than a small one, and nothing against an empty one.

The **friendly robber** limits you. While a player has 2 or fewer visible victory points you can't put the robber on any hex touching their buildings, so there is no steal either. Hidden VP cards don't count as visible, so an opponent can look protected while holding points. If no legal hex is left, the robber still has to move; it just can't take anything.

You are protected the same way, so early 7s are mostly about parking the robber somewhere that costs you nothing.
