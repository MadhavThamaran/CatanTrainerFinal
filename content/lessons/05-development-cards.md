# The deck and its rules

A development card costs one ore, one wheat and one sheep. The deck has 25 cards: **14 knights, 5 victory points, 2 Road Building, 2 Year of Plenty and 2 Monopoly**.

Three rules to remember:

- You can't play a card on the turn you bought it.
- You can play only one non-VP card per turn.
- Victory-point cards are never played. They stay hidden, count toward your 15 the moment you hold them, and don't count as *visible* points, so holding them doesn't end friendly-robber protection.

---
# Knights and Largest Army

Knights are more than half the deck. Playing one moves the robber and steals a card, like a 7 but without the discards, and every played knight counts toward **Largest Army**. Three played knights earn 2 victory points, and the award passes to whoever has played strictly more. A tie keeps it with the holder.

That makes knights a race you can count, because played knights are public. If you have two and your opponent has one, your third knight takes the award. If you both have two, whoever plays the next one takes it. Before you spend a knight on the robber, check whether holding it for the award is worth more.

---
# The other four

**Road Building** gives two free roads (you need at least one legal road to play it): the cheap way to reach a spot or to take Longest Road.

**Year of Plenty** takes any two resources from the bank. It completes a build you were two cards short of.

**Monopoly** names a resource and takes every card of it from your opponent. Their hand size is public, so it's strongest when they are sitting on a pile of one resource.

Victory-point cards change the endgame count. You only see your opponent's *visible* points, but they may be hiding more. When you count the race, treat them as their visible total plus the unplayed development cards they could be holding.
